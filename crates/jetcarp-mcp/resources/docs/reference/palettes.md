# Palettes

A palette is a list of colour *stops* plus two more colours: the *background*, for empty cells, and
the *stage*, for the area outside the tiling. Each automaton in a scene has its own palette, and the
rule decides how its states and ages are spread along the stops. JETCARP has 75 built-in palettes;
`jetcarp palettes` lists them, and `jetcarp palettes --json` gives the same list as data. For choosing
and editing palettes in the app, see [Palettes and colouring](../guides/palettes.md).

## The built-in palettes

<p class="generated-note">Generated from <code>jetcarp palettes --json</code> by
<code>docs/tools/gen-palettes.py</code>, which CI checks against the program. The bar is each palette's ramp, drawn as JETCARP interpolates it: in a straight line between neighbouring
stops, on the palette's own background. The last column is the program's own
<a href="#colour-vision-deficiency">check for colour-vision deficiency</a>.</p>

Candy, Glacier, Desert, Paper, Riso, Vellum, Linen, Ink Wash, Bluebell, Fern, Brick, Mulberry and
Driftwood have light backgrounds; the rest are dark. Stained Glass, Opal, Tidewheel and Compass Rose
are closed colour wheels whose last stop repeats the first, so the cyclic automaton and
rock-paper-scissors, which colour states around the whole palette, show no seam. Some palettes, such
as Verdigris, end on a bright stop, so a pattern that has settled stays vivid under age colouring.
Lantern, Cobalt and Saffron are chosen for colour-vision deficiency: Lantern for any rule, and Cobalt
(+1) and Saffron (−1) for JACA's two sides.

## Choosing a palette

- **In a scene**, each automaton's `palette` field names its palette (see the
  [scene file format](scene-format.md#automata-cas)). A JACA automaton has a second one,
  `palette_neg`, for its −1 cells. The defaults are Ember, and for JACA Tide (+1) and Koi (−1), which stay apart at every age.
  In the app, *Settings → Accessibility* can choose other defaults for scenes that name none, such
  as the palettes chosen for colour-vision deficiency (see
  [Accessibility settings](../guides/accessibility-settings.md#palettes-for-scenes-that-name-none)).
- **On the command line**, commands that draw pictures, such as `jetcarp wallpaper`,
  `jetcarp hashlife`, `jetcarp sheet` and `jetcarp search pile`, take `--palette NAME`.
- **Your own colours.** Wherever a palette name is accepted you can also write colours: `#4fc3ff`
  is one colour, and `#103050-#4fc3ff` a ramp from the first to the last. Stops are separated by
  `-`, commas or spaces, young or low first, as three- or six-digit hex. A scene can also carry named
  palettes of its own in its `palettes` list, each with its stops and, optionally, its own background
  and stage; the app saves the palettes you make there.

Names are matched exactly, capitals included: the scene's own palettes first, then the built-in
ones, then hex colours. A name you type that matches none of them (`ember`, say), in a `--palette`
option, a command or a script, is refused with the list of names. A scene file that names one still
loads, so that a scene saved by a later JETCARP with newer palettes opens: the name is kept (saving
the scene keeps it), that automaton is drawn in Ember, and `jetcarp run`, `jetcarp verify` and the
app's status line say so ("palette 'Dusk' is unknown here; drawn in Ember").

## Colour-vision deficiency

JETCARP tells cells' states and ages apart by colour, so it checks how each built-in palette's
colours look to people with the common kinds of colour-vision deficiency. Each stop is seen with
normal vision, and as simulated for protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia and for their milder
forms, protanomaly, deuteranomaly and tritanomaly, with the model of
[Machado, Oliveira and Fernandes 2009](../research/bibliography.md#machado2009physiologically)
(severity 1 for the first three, 0.5 for the milder ones, on linear RGB). The colours are then
compared with the CIEDE2000 colour difference
([Sharma, Wu and Dalal 2005](../research/bibliography.md#sharma2005ciede2000)). A palette passes
when, seen all seven ways, every pair of neighbouring stops, and every stop against the palette's
background, keep a CIEDE2000 difference of at least 10.

Why 10: CIEDE2000 is scaled so that a difference of about 1 is just noticeable between two large
patches side by side. Cells are small, often a few pixels, and the colours to tell apart are
scattered over the grid rather than side by side, which needs differences several times larger
([Szafir 2018](../research/bibliography.md#szafir2018modeling)); and the simulation models an
average observer, while people with the same kind of deficiency differ. Ten just-noticeable
differences leave that margin.

The table above gives each palette's result: *Passes*, with the smallest difference found, or the
stops that come closest and for which kind. 49 of the 75 pass. Three were chosen first:

- **Lantern**, for any rule: yellow through green and teal to blue, a ramp of the kind of
  [cividis](../research/bibliography.md#nunez2018optimizing) and viridis whose lightness falls
  steadily from the first stop to the last, so that lightness alone tells the stops apart whatever
  a deficiency takes away.
- **Cobalt** and **Saffron**, for JACA's +1 and −1 cells: blue and orange, the two that every kind
  of deficiency keeps apart. Each falls steadily in lightness too, every stop of one stays at least
  10 from every stop of the other (cells of either sign, of any ages, meet), and both stay clear of
  the pale yellow glow of annihilated cells. Tide and Koi, the JACA defaults, pass one by one but
  come within 2 of each other under deuteranopia.

42 more were designed to pass, so that whatever look you want there is a palette for it that
works for every kind of deficiency. Each one's lightness runs one way, at least 12 L* a step, so
lightness carries it; hue and chroma give it its character:

- **Ramps of several hues** on the dark slate, light to dark: Dusk (yellow, orange, rose, violet,
  indigo), Furnace, Kelp, Abyss, Rosewood, Harbour (from yellow through grey to navy, the cividis
  kind) and Lagoon.
- **One hue each** on the dark slate: Heather, Graphite, Honey, Spruce, Fjord, Copper, Peony,
  Sulphur, Frost and Sepia.
- **Longer ramps** on a deeper ground: Midnight Garden (six stops), Lava Flow and Nebula (six
  stops).
- **On a light ground**, young cells darkest: Vellum, Linen, Ink Wash, Bluebell, Fern, Brick,
  Mulberry and Driftwood.
- **Closed wheels for cyclic rules** (Greenberg-Hastings, the cyclic automaton, rock-paper-
  scissors), whose last stop is their first: Tidewheel and Compass Rose. They go down in lightness
  through cool hues and back up through warm ones, so neighbouring stops differ in lightness, and
  stops of one lightness differ as blue does from orange.
- **Pairs for JACA**, +1 then −1, held to the same tests as Cobalt and Saffron: Indigo and
  Marmalade, Sky and Rust, Iris and Gold, Lapis and Amber, Ice and Ember Red, and Delft and Ochre.
  All are cool against warm, the difference every kind of deficiency keeps.

They were designed with
[`docs/tools/design-palettes.py`](https://github.com/jprodgers/jetcarp/blob/main/docs/tools/design-palettes.py):
ramps in OKLCH, through the lightness steps CIEDE2000 needs (larger near white and black), and the
same check as the program's. The program's check is the judge.

Tide, Phosphor, Koi and Verdigris pass as well. Ink fails by design: its first two stops are the
same colour, so newborn and young cells look alike. The check is
[`jetcarp_core::cvd`](https://github.com/jprodgers/jetcarp/blob/main/crates/jetcarp-core/src/cvd.rs),
and the test `crates/jetcarp-core/tests/palettes_cvd.rs` holds every passing palette, and every
JACA pair (`cvd::JACA_PAIRS`), to it. A
simulation is not a person: if you have a colour-vision deficiency and a palette doesn't work for
you, please [say so](https://github.com/jprodgers/jetcarp/issues/new/choose).

## How a palette colours cells

Every automaton has a *colour by* setting (`color_by` in a scene):

| Setting | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| `auto` (the default) | The rule's natural choice, in the table below |
| `age` | How long each cell has been in its state (see [Age colouring](#age-colouring)) |
| `state` | One colour per state: state 0 is the background and states 1 and up are spread evenly from the first stop to the last |
| `row` | Each live cell by its height in the picture, top to bottom, across the whole palette |

Sandpiles are always coloured by height, and continuous fields (Lenia) by value, unless coloured by
row. With `auto`, each family is coloured like this:

| Family | Colours |
| --- | --- |
| Life-like, isotropic, Generations, Larger than Life, priority tables, Margolus, and the two-state row and layered rules | By age. Row and layered rules write each cell once, so their cells keep the first stop |
| JACA | +1 cells by age from `palette`, −1 cells by age from `palette_neg`; destroyed cells glow pale yellow over the background and fade (`glow`, on by default; drawing only, not part of the state) |
| Generations' dying states, and states above 1 of other multi-state rules coloured by age | By state, between 45% and 95% of the way along the palette |
| Cyclic automaton, rock-paper-scissors | Every state, 0 included, at an even step around the palette |
| Hodgepodge | Every state from 0 at the first stop to the highest at the last |
| Greenberg–Hastings | Resting cells are background, excited cells the first stop, refractory stages from about a third of the way along towards the end |
| Ants and turmites | Colour 0 is background, colours 1 and up spread from the first stop; the ants themselves are drawn on top in a fixed pink-red (`#ff3b6b`) |
| Three-state hexagon rules, rule tables | State 0 is background, states 1 and up spread from the first stop to the last |
| Spectre glider | States 1, 2 and 3 at the start, 80% and 50% of the way along |
| Sandpile | Height 0 is background; heights 1 up to one below the tile's threshold are spread from the first stop; a tile still at or above its threshold shows the last stop |
| Lenia | Empty cells are background; low values take the last stop and a value of 1 the first |
| WireWorld | Fixed colours: electron heads blue, tails red, wire amber |
| Forest fire | Fixed colours: trees green, fire orange |
| Von Neumann's 29 states | A fixed colour for each state (see [Von Neumann's automaton](../automata/von-neumann.md#notation-and-display)) |

### Several automata

Each field is coloured by the first automaton in the scene's list that uses it, and fields are drawn
in order, later ones over earlier ones, with ants on top. Empty cells take the background of the palette of the first
automaton on the bottom field, and the area outside the tiling takes its stage colour; tile outlines
are a shade of that background. See [Several automata at once](../guides/several-automata.md).

## Age colouring

Each live cell carries an *age*: the number of generations it has been in its current state,
starting at 1 when it is born or changes state (the count stops at about 65,000). Under age colouring a
newborn cell takes the first stop, and older cells move along the palette on a logarithmic scale,
reaching the last stop at an age of 400. A still life therefore ends at the last stop, an oscillator
flickers near the start, and the debris of an old soup shows its history: recently active regions at
the young end, settled ones at the old end. Every stop of the built-in palettes is chosen to contrast
with the palette's background, since any of them can end up next to an empty cell.

The ages are part of the world's state hash (see [Reproducibility](../research/reproducibility.md)).
HashLife jumps keep the cells but not their ages: after a jump every live cell has age 1.

JETCARP makes no claim for age colouring: Chris Rowett's LifeViewer and other programs colour cells by
age, and fade cells that die, and did so first (see
[What is new in JETCARP](../research/contributions.md#what-we-do-not-claim)).
